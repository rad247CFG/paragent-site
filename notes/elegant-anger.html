Elegant Anger

A Third Thought on the only tool we are trained to fire or forget

Ask almost anybody whether they can choose not to be angry about something and they will say yes. Not always, not about everything, but often enough that the answer comes out without hesitation. People say I decided to stop being angry about that and I'm choosing not to let this get to me, and they mean it, and they are usually reporting accurately.

Hold onto that, because almost nobody unpacks what it implies.

The bank Fuckwit

My father had died. The estate work fell to me as an act of care for my mother. Most of it required my mother physically present in a branch holding original documents and me dealing with the bureaucracy that a death in the family attracts. I drove ninety minutes to her place. I waited an hour while she got ready. We queued ten minutes. By that point I had made three phone calls to the bank, visited two branches, obtained the death certificate and located the will. The bank forms we needed to get Mum access to the credit card she had shared with Dad were printed, completed and in my hand.

What I needed from the woman at the counter was for her to look at three original documents, confirm they were what they claimed to be, and stamp the copies and accept the printed signed forms. Five minutes of work. She was authorised to do it and the stamp was on her desk.

She said I needed an appointment. She said the process had to take an hour in-branch with a specialist. Nobody in three phone calls and two prior branch visits had mentioned an appointment. And it was simply Fuckwittery to expect the process to take an hour in branch if the online forms were done already and could be submitted online. We were here to avoid the problems of finding a JP and to speed up the mailing of the copies.

I got angry with the Fuckwit. Not a raised eyebrow and a firm tone. Angry, loudly, in public, in front of my mother, because I was dealing with all the crap that comes from the paper pushers and compliance Fuckwits after your father dies.

In the end she certified the documents but refused to accept the will because it was a copy not the original. The hour-long process was not mentioned again. We were out in twenty minutes but with only part success. My mother still could not use the credit card. She was upset by the scene and told me so. I had to justify why I got angry. Later that day she told me she thought she understood why I had gotten angry and that it probably was necessary to get progress.

I told the story as the bank messing us around four times to justify how I had acted. Except that standing at that counter I was not counting anything. I was already gone. The four visits came into the story afterwards, when I needed to explain — to other people, and to myself — why I had carried on like a pork chop in a bank branch in front of my mother. The reasons arrived to defend the anger. They did not cause it.

Once you notice the reasons arriving late you have to ask what was actually doing the work.

Counter Agency

Two things were true at that counter and I had them welded together.

The first was live. This woman, right now, has the discretion to solve this in two minutes.

The second was history. Here is everything it cost me to get here.

Only one of those was something she could do anything about. She could not give me back the phone calls. She could not undo two wasted branch visits or the drive or the hour in my mother's kitchen. The single thing in front of her that she could change was whether the stamp came down, and that was the only part of what I said to her that had anywhere to land. Everything else I said was for me.

History is what feels like the argument. It is the part with the weight in it that moves nobody except me.

Lose-Lose

Run the counterfactual. Suppose I had not got angry.

I would have accepted we had to come back after getting an appointment. I would have driven ninety minutes home with nothing done, my mother would have had a fortnight longer in the administrative limbo that follows a death, and I would have come back on a Tuesday to be told something else. Compliance was not a path to the outcome (and in my experience after a process delay meek compliance prevents any good outcome for me). It was a path to a second attempt at the outcome, going to cost another day of wasted time.

Everybody recognises extreme anger fails, because it costs you standing and burns whoever is beside you and requires a justification you have to build afterwards. But not-anger also fails, because the barrier does not move. Two options, both lose, pick your poison and call it maturity.

I sat in that dilemma for years. It is fake, and it is fake because of a premise so quiet I never inspected it: that anger has one setting.

Digital, analogue or elegant

Go back to the concession at the top. If you can choose to not be angry, maybe the anger state has a dial, and your hand has been on it the whole time.

Digital anger has two positions. On and off. The only skill anybody is taught is holding it at off for as long as possible, and the training starts early and is delivered by people who mean well. Don't raise your voice. Don't make a scene. Don't be so aggressive. Calm down. Every one of those is an instruction about position and none of them is an instruction about control, so when the state does fire it fires at whatever setting it picks, which is maximum, because nobody has ever practised anything else.

Analogue anger has a range. You can be too angry and you can also be not angry enough. Miffed is less than frustrated is less than angry is less than outraged is less than enraged is less than berserk is less than psycho. Somewhere on that scale is the level this situation actually calls for, and it is almost never the top and it is frequently not the bottom either. Analogue is the recognition that the scale exists and that a level can be adjusted up or down. Everyone has had the experience of being angry and then getting even angrier before finally calming down somewhat.

Elegant anger is analogue plus aim. Volitional, calibrated, and pointed at an outcome with more than one component in it: the thing I came here to get done, the respect owed in both directions, and an increase in the agency of everybody at the counter including the Fuckwit processor who is making me angry. Elegance is not restraint. It is not a quieter anger. It is anger that has been set rather than issued, sized to a job, focused with precision and finished when the job is done.

Calibration is not merely moderation. If a situation warrants a large response, large is correct and large is still elegant. The dial is not a virtue scale where lower scores better.

And the volition is the point. Digital anger is issued to you at default scale by a process you did not author. Elegant anger is set by you. That is a difference of mode, not degree, which is why be angry but not too angry is not what I am describing and is in fact the same old taboo in a nicer jacket.

By my own definition it is also more agency, not less. The primal state deciding versus you deciding. It happens to limit the damage to whoever is standing beside you, which in the bank was my mother. I wish I had been elegantly angry rather than digitally angry when I was trying to help my Mum in the bank that day. I lost sight of some of the things that mattered.

What I was actually demanding

Standing at that counter I wanted my earned status acknowledged. The doctorate, the companies, the fact that I had done the entire diagnostic myself and knew precisely which two minutes of her job I needed. I wanted that processor to register that the man in front of her was not the ordinary problem in the queue. I wanted respect: which is a ridiculous ego problem to take into a bank to resolve because no one I know goes to the bank to get respect!

Stephen Darwall's distinction, drawn in 1977 and mostly unread outside philosophy departments, is that respect comes in two kinds that behave completely differently. Recognition respect is owed to persons as persons. It constrains how you may treat someone, it does not scale with anything, and everybody has an equal claim on it, including her.

Appraisal respect tracks merit. It is domain-bound and it exists only where the merit can be read. There are certainly places where merit acknowledgement is owed — nobody thinks the respect due to firefighters who died trying to save homes is optional — but a bank counter is not one of them, and not by accident. Procedure at a counter exists precisely so that the person standing in front of the clerk cannot convert who they are into what they get. That is the design intent, not a defect in it.

So, I had carried a merit claim to a window built to be incapable of paying it. An inexhaustible supply of such windows exists in my world. I had a chronic ego condition and had been treating it as a series of episodic lapses.

Prestige is conferred freely. It survives your leaving the room. It cannot be taken by force. Dominance is coerced and it evaporates the moment you are out of sight. Both are status and they do not clear against each other.

I was carrying a prestige need into a status-blind venue and settling it with dominance. I won those encounters. Not one of them closed anything. I learnt not to try to collect needed currency at the wrong window.

The completion condition

The practical instrument is one question, asked before engaging.

What behaviour, from this person, would end this anger state?

In the bank, certify these three copies and be civil while you do it is practical. That has an answer you can see arrive, and when it arrives the thing is finished and nothing carries.

Now try the other one. Acknowledge me. The trap here is not that it fails to complete. It completed — she certified the documents, she dropped the process, she was careful with me for the rest of the transaction. It looked exactly like acknowledgement. But what I had actually produced was a revised estimate, in her, of the cost of ignoring me. That is real and it is durable and it contains no recognition whatsoever, because recognition extracted under pressure is not recognition. It likely evaporated as I walked out.

Which is why the demand never settles and why the pressure kept building through decades of encounters I often won. She can do every single thing I ask and I still will not have what I came for, because what I came for is for her to think something about me, and thinking is not on the list of things you can make somebody do. Compliance is all that pressure produces. Compliance is not it. So the dial has nothing to size itself against, and intensity drifts up to fill the gap, and it drifts up again next time. If any one of those encounters had actually delivered what my ego was after, the tank might have drained. Ask the question honestly enough times and the status demand does not need to be nobly renounced. It simply fails, every time, to produce a behaviour you could point at. It starves.

The small one

The best I have done is very small and I keep returning to it. Another employee at a company where I am a director was disrespectful to me the first time we met, years ago. Recently, consulted about manufacturing requirements for a systems build, he said in front of a colleague that it was not up to him to teach me about manufacturing.

I was mildly angry and I chose it. "I will not tolerate your disrespect. You have no idea what I know about manufacturing." That was all of it. A rap on the nose for a puppy that just bit.

He became minimally helpful and civil, which I judged to be everything realistically available from him, and I took it. The relationship continued. Nobody was diminished. Precise completion condition, response sized to it, over in one sentence.

That one has residue in how I tell it. I am proud that I did elegantly what few others in my company have been able to do with an employee we all feel is difficult to get along with. I do attach a lingering status claim of my skill for myself. It is the most interesting to me emotionally.

Note the direction of that, though, because it matters. The claim arrived afterwards, out of having handled the thing well, and I never asked him for a scrap of it. That is a different animal from carrying a claim into a bank and presenting it at the counter for payment. Residue you generate yourself is cheap and it costs the other person nothing. Residue you demand from somebody else is the whole problem.

Contrast this to a motorbike accident. A woman pulled out of a driveway, I laid the bike down to avoid her, her liability was never in question. Her insurer's processor told me I could not choose my own repairer because that was not their process. I was mildly angry and I was correct: I am not your customer, I am a third party in tort, you do not get to direct method or repairer, and if you underpay I will sue your client for the balance. They folded, then ran a photo review afterwards to save face.

That one has no residue at all in how I tell it, no history attached, no status claim, nothing lingering. It is the cleanest case I have and it is the least interesting to me emotionally, which is itself the evidence.

Model broken

I had assumed this ego thing was about status not being acknowledged. Two cases killed that. A genuine peer disrespected me at a board table, in a venue where my standing was entirely legible, in front of people who knew exactly what I had done. I was not angry. I was dominated, and I judged that anger in the room would have made it worse. A potential customer dismissed me loudly on first meeting, because of a flat and almost religious bias about large firms, and cost me the chance at business I really needed. I was not angry then either. I was sad, and I keenly felt the unfairness of it. Those were real verdicts, from people who had assessed me and reached a conclusion, in places where the merit claim was perfectly readable. Both produced no anger despite the unfairness.

A counter clerk who has formed no view of me at all, who is applying the same frame to whoever is next in the queue, produces the most anger I have ever produced. If this were about status, that is backwards. So it can't be about status.

The disowning

It is not that she could not help me. It is that she would not own the interaction, and the disowning is what arrives as disrespect. Being dismissed. Being snubbed by somebody who has not even looked up.

A processor who says the rules require this, I know what you need, I am not allowed, here is who can draws no anger from me. She has named her constraint honestly, handed me the escalation path, and stayed a person on my side of the counter. She often has less latitude than that bank teller did. She is not the problem.

A processor who says the system will not allow it and stops has done something different. Same words, nearly. Opposite stance. She has positioned herself as a conduit and there is now nobody in the room who is the author of anything.

The clearest one I ever got was at a transport department window, where a Fuckwit clerk refused my payment because I could not prove to her satisfaction that I was a director of the company that owned the car, while I stood there holding her own department's letter addressed to me in that capacity. She told me she was trying to help. She was not helping. She was following procedure without thinking, uninterested in the fact that I was attempting to give her department money, and more concerned with avoiding accountability for using any discretion.

She had the discretion. What she did not have was any appetite for owning it, because her employer has made ownership career-negative and has built the role so that authorship is nobody's.

I was not elegantly angry there. I was plain angry, at full scale, and security asked me to leave. I did not pay the registration that day, I did not get my dignity either, and I had to come back and do the entire thing again with somebody else. That is the one I would most like to have back, and it is the strongest evidence in this essay. Everything I have been describing was present. The discretion was real. The letter was in my hand. The trigger was clean and my read of her was correct in every particular. Digital anger converted all of that into a walk to the car park. Being right is not the instrument. Aim is the instrument.

Compare a private seller I bought slot car track from recently. It arrived with corrosion on it, described as new. She could not have known — the pack was sealed and she had never opened it. She apologised, offered a full refund on return or a partial refund if I kept it, and did it within the hour. No obligation and no fault, and she took responsibility anyway. I told her not to worry about it and cleaned the track myself over the weekend with sandpaper and a can of Inox. No anger. None available. She was the one who had let me down and she drew nothing, which tells you the trigger is not friction, not cost, and not being let down.

Note there is a second error running the other way at the bank counter, and it isn't mine. The Fuckwit processor who refused to help me has accepted the system's appraisal of herself. She has concluded that somebody in her role is owed no standing and therefore holds no discretion, and you cannot extend recognition to another person if you have already declined to hold it for yourself. Two perceptual errors meeting at one counter. Mine, that I deserve respect for what I have done. Hers, that she is a blind worker drone. Both wrong, and both of us owed better, by each other and by ourselves.

Calibrated anger is the only move I have found that corrects both, because dropping mine is what makes it possible to read hers.

The lines

None of this requires a scene, and that is the part I most want to get across, because "calibrated anger" sounds like a discipline you would need to train for. It is not. The whole technique fits in one sentence and most of the useful sentences are barely warm.

"I will not tolerate your disrespect. You have no idea what I know about manufacturing." Small, aimed, over. The completion condition was civility and civility arrived.

"I have respected you so far, as that sign over there demands. Don't you think you should do the same for me?" Most of these counters have a sign about how their staff are to be treated. Point at it. It makes the reciprocity explicit, it uses the institution's own posted rule, and it is very hard to answer with procedure because it is not a procedural question.

"If you are unable to help me, you could at least say so, and then help me escalate to someone who can, surely?" This one hands her the honest-processor script. It names the two things she can actually do and it lets her out of a position she probably did not want. Often she takes it.

"So having worked all the way through the various layers to escalate to you, the person who can fix this, you are saying I am going to have to leave without a solution?" That was an Amazon manager three levels up who had just told me the buck stopped with him and I was not getting a solution. I repeated his own claim back to him with the consequence attached. He found another escalation.

"What is the purpose of your customer support role, if it is not about helping me solve my problem?" and, when both moves are being blocked at once, "Is there a reason you are trying to both avoid fixing my issue and stop me getting to someone who would fix it?"

Look at the temperature on those. The Amazon one contains no anger in the words at all. It is a mirror. Buck-stops-here is a thing people say before they have heard themselves say it, and hearing it said back is enough. The escalation line is barely a raised eyebrow. Only the first one has any real heat in the language, and that is because the situation called for heat.

Calibrated does not mean loud. It means the level is chosen and the sentence is aimed, and most of the time the level that works is far lower than the one that would have fired by itself.

Aikido

Two things are going on in those sentences and both of them matter.

The first is that not one of them is my weight. Every one redirects something the counterpart or their system has already put into the room. The posted sign about how staff are to be treated. The stated purpose of a customer support function. The manager's own claim that the buck stops with him. The plain incompatibility of refusing to fix a thing and refusing to let anybody else fix it either. I bring almost nothing. I take what the institution has already committed to and let it carry the counterpart somewhere they were not intending to go, and the reason it works is that they cannot disown any of it without disowning the role they are standing in.

That is the frame move, and it is the whole game. Not winning the argument inside the frame. Refusing the frame and using their inertia to do it.

The second thing is tone, and it is why none of this is a script. My words in these exchanges often look mild on the page. My tone does not. The counterpart hears activated negative affect aimed squarely at them, and that is what makes the sentence land rather than read as a debating point. A warm delivery turns the Amazon line into a joke. A flat delivery turns it into a complaint that he can process and file. The state has to be present and it has to be legible, because the state is the part he cannot argue with.

Which brings the two halves together, and this is the actual reason to calibrate.

Anger supplies activation. That is what it is for, and the counterpart has to feel it or nothing changes.

But the frame move requires generating an option that is not on the menu, and high arousal destroys exactly that capacity. The angry man is right about the fight and blind to the room. He is excellent at execution and terrible at option generation, because he chose the fight before he looked around. At the bank I could have walked out to the car park and escalated by phone, away from my mother, and it might well have worked. That option existed the entire time and was completely invisible from inside the state.

So the state that gets you to engage is the same state that removes the move worth making. Calibration is not primarily about proportion, and it is not mainly about sparing whoever is standing beside you, though it does that too. It is the condition under which the aikido is still available. Too hot and there is no aikido left, only the fight you were already in, which is the fight the system is designed to win.

Elegance is holding both at once. Enough heat that they feel it. Enough head that you can still see what to redirect.

Please do this

You have three options at your version of the bank counter with the unhelpful Fuckwit. Submit meekly and perpetuate the agency erosion in the interaction. Go postal and probably make it worse. Or apply calibrated anger and put system cost back the other way — nudge the processor in front of you the way the system has taught her to nudge you.

The third one works even when it fails. Say nothing changes today. The cost still lands. She has an unpleasant afternoon that she reports to nobody and her employer never sees, and if that is the only instance, nothing happens. But if we all do it at scale, then eventually she changes or leaves. Attrition is legible to an employer in a way that a complaint form is not, because it arrives as vacancy cost and recruitment spend and requires nobody to report anything. That is the only channel by which the design of these counters has ever changed.

Fehr and Gächter have the shape of it. Costly punishment restores cooperation in groups, people will pay real money to impose a cost on a defector with no prospect of personal return, and the cooperation collapses when the punishment is removed. The catch in their work is that the punishment is public and the norm is legible to everybody. Elegant anger is often private, one counter at a time, possibly invisible to the next person in the queue or the next teller over. Which is exactly why these encounters are being documented here.

But if we all do it enough, then respect at these counters becomes automatic rather than extracted, which is better for everybody including her. And look at what the interaction actually is while it is running. My agency goes up. Hers goes up, because she exercises latitude she had been treating as non-existent. It presents as QIV and becomes QI with the application of calibrated anger.

A correction

In Shruggery I wrote that the Processor does not fear your anger, that anger is loud and exhausting and burns itself out on schedule. True of digital anger, and I should have said so. Loud, undirected, retrospectively justified anger is no threat to an extractive system. It is priced in. It arguably forms part of the product, because it discredits whoever is carrying it and resolves nothing. A Processor who has absorbed a hundred of them has learned that the storm passes and the policy holds.

Calibrated anger is a different instrument. Aimed at a specified behaviour, terminates when that behaviour arrives, costs the person opposite something real, leaves the relationship standing. It does not burn out on schedule because it was never lit for longer than the job.

Robert Frank's Passions Within Reason is the real objection and it belongs in the open. His argument is that anger works as a commitment device precisely because it is involuntary — the threat is credible because you visibly cannot back down. A dial can be discounted, and somebody who learns you have one can learn to wait you out. I think it survives, because elegant anger is still real anger, sized, rather than a performance of anger. But it means the repeated relationship, where somebody watches you do this more than once, is the exposed case. My best example is also my most fragile.

What is owed

I do not need the clerk to respect the doctorate or the income or the role. That was never available at that window and demanding it there is what made everything else worse. I need her to respect my humanity, to be considerate of what I am trying to achieve, and to do the job in front of her, even minimally. All three are behaviour. None of them scale with rank. All of them are things that pressure can actually produce.

And the last one has a shape I missed for twenty years. She is a person and so am I, and ignoring somebody standing in front of you is a choice rather than an absence. She expects to be seen by me. She is declining to see me in return. That argument needs no status at all, it runs in both directions, and it does not depend on the system's frame for a second, because she has her own agency and could simply choose to be human with it.

We are trained never to aim our anger. We are told to hold anger at zero. Which works until finally we cannot, and then it fires at maximum, and we build the story afterwards to explain why we were justified in carrying on like a pork chop.

Put your hand on the dial. Ask what behaviour ends this. Set it where it needs to be, which is sometimes very low and occasionally not, and be finished the moment the thing you asked for arrives.

We all have skin in this game, and your part matters.
